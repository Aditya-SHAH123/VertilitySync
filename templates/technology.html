Overview/Technology

From a stack of
images to a volume.

Every stage of the reconstruction is documented, including what causes it to stop. Nothing here is a trained model — the method is classical image processing, and it is labelled as such everywhere it appears.

The pipeline

Eight stages, and what stops each one.

Every stage consumes the previous stage's output, so a failure part-way through halts the run rather than producing a degraded result.

01

Read the series

Multi-file DICOM upload, parsed entirely in memory.

A file that cannot be decoded is marked FAIL individually; the remaining slices still proceed.

02

Validate

Sixteen per-file and series-level checks covering identity, geometry, and pixel data.

Structural failures — missing pixel data, inconsistent Rows/Columns, fewer than two usable slices — block reconstruction. Warnings do not.

03

Order the slices

Each slice's position is projected onto the slice normal derived from the image orientation cosines. Filenames are never used.

Without usable geometry it falls back to SliceLocation, then InstanceNumber, and flags which was used.

04

Build the volume

Slices are stacked into a single [slice, row, column] array at float32.

Slices whose dimensions disagree with the majority are excluded and reported.

05

Convert to Hounsfield units

Rescale slope and intercept are read from each slice individually, never assumed constant across the series.

A slice missing either value keeps its raw values and is marked HU unavailable rather than estimated.

06

Isolate the lung region

Body mask, HU thresholding, and 3D connected-component analysis — a rule-based method, not a trained model.

If the result is not anatomically plausible the run stops here. See the thresholds below.

07

Reconstruct the surface

Marching cubes at true physical voxel spacing, producing vertices directly in patient-space millimetres.

An empty or completely filled mask has no extractable surface and is refused.

08

Render

Surface mesh, direct volume rendering of the original voxel intensities, or both together.

Interactive and high-fidelity are separate representations; the simplified one is never presented as the reference.

Slice ordering

Filenames are never used.

A DICOM series arrives as an unordered set of files. Sorting them by name is the most common way to get a reconstruction subtly, invisibly wrong — scanners do not guarantee that filename order matches anatomical order.

MethodUsed whenReported as

The preferred method takes the cross product of the row and column direction cosines to get the slice normal, then projects each slice's patient-space position onto it and sorts by that scalar. This is robust to reversed input order and to randomised filenames, because it uses only physical position.

When a fallback is used it is flagged in the study summary and surfaced in the viewer, and left/right lung labelling is skipped entirely — without reliable orientation there is no defensible way to say which side is which.

Hounsfield conversion

Per slice, never assumed.

Stored pixel values are scanner-specific integers. They become Hounsfield units through HU = value × RescaleSlope + RescaleIntercept, and both terms are read from each slice's own dataset rather than sampled once from the first slice.

That distinction matters because a series can legitimately carry different rescale parameters per slice. Assuming they are constant produces a volume that looks correct and is quietly wrong in density — which would then propagate into every threshold decision the segmentation makes.

Where either value is absent, that slice keeps its raw stored values and is marked HU-unavailable. The study summary then reports conversion as AVAILABLE, PARTIAL, or NOT AVAILABLE, and the viewer shows “unavailable” instead of a number when you inspect a voxel on such a slice.

Segmentation, stated plainly

It is not AI, and it says so.

Lung extraction uses classical 3D image processing: external-air removal to isolate the body silhouette, Hounsfield thresholding to find air-density regions inside it, 26-connectivity component analysis, volume-based noise filtering, and hole filling. No trained model is involved, and the method is reported verbatim in every response.

When it refuses rather than guesses

  • Calibrated Hounsfield units are available for fewer than 90% of slices.
  • No air-density voxels are found inside the body silhouette.
  • Body-silhouette isolation produces an empty mask.
  • The segmented volume falls below 200 mL, or exceeds 60% of the body silhouette.
  • No candidate region meets the minimum component volume.

Left and right

The lungs are usually one connected region.

On a real study the two lungs are almost never separate components. The trachea and main bronchi carry air at the same density as the lung fields, so a connectivity analysis joins them into a single region.

A

Two comparable components

Treated as the two lungs directly, with the side assigned from the patient coordinate frame.

Requires the runner-up to be at least 30% of the largest — a much smaller region is something else, not a lung.

B

One merged component

Split at the sagittal midline, located as the narrowest point of the per-column profile — the mediastinum — rather than the mask centroid, which drifts when one lung is larger.

The division near the midline is approximate, and the result says so.

C

Unreliable orientation

Left and right are not labelled at all.

Guessing a side without a trustworthy patient frame would be worse than declining.

Measured on a real study

  • Before these rules: 197 mL and 6,012 mL — a balance of 0.03, with a gas bubble labelled as a whole lung.
  • After: 2,350 mL and 2,618 mL — a balance of 0.90, with the right lung slightly larger, as expected anatomically.
