Overview/Safety & Limits

A tool is more useful
when its limits are explicit.

This page exists because the honest answer to “what can it do?” includes everything it cannot.

Boundaries

What it deliberately does not do.

A visualization tool is more useful when its limits are explicit than when they are implied.

Not implemented, and not claimed

  • Disease classification of any kind
  • Fibrosis, ground-glass, or nodule detection
  • Lobe segmentation and airway or vessel trees
  • Abnormality probability or heat maps
  • Radiology-report or clinical-note analysis
  • Treatment recommendation or clinical decisions
  • HIPAA compliance, FDA clearance, or clinical validation

How imaging data is handled

Uploaded files are parsed in memory. Only an explicit allow-list of non-identifying technical fields is ever read from a dataset:

ModalityManufacturerManufacturerModelName KVPSliceThicknessPhotometricInterpretation

Patient name, identifiers, birth date, and accession number are never accessed, displayed, or stored. No imaging data is sent to any external service, including any AI or media-generation service. Every anatomical image on this page is synthetic and drawn procedurally in your browser.

Known limitations

Where it is not yet reliable.

Stated plainly, because a tool whose weaknesses are documented is safer than one whose weaknesses are discovered.

01

Segmentation is threshold-based

It selects air-density regions inside the body. Anything sharing that density range can be included, and pathology that changes lung density can cause under-inclusion.

It has not been validated against expert manual segmentation. No accuracy figure is claimed.

02

The left/right division is approximate

When the lungs form one connected region through the airway, they are separated at the sagittal midline rather than by true anatomical boundary.

Per-lung volumes near the mediastinum should be treated as estimates.

03

Lobes, airways, and vessels are not separated

The mask is a single lung region. Internal holes are filled to produce a solid, meshable surface.

Structures inside the lung are not individually identified.

04

Large studies are memory-intensive

Peak memory runs at roughly seven times the reconstructed volume, so a 400-slice study approaches three gigabytes during segmentation.

Very large series may exceed available memory on constrained hosts.

05

Measurements are session-only

Distances taken in the viewer are computed from physical coordinates but are not saved to the case.

Persistent measurement is not yet implemented.

06

Point selection needs a surface

Selecting a location resolves by intersecting the lung surface, so it is unavailable in volume-only rendering.

The viewer says so rather than ignoring the click.

Questions

Before you import a study.

What exactly do I need to upload?

The original DICOM series — the folder of .dcm files a scanner produces, typically hundreds of them for a chest CT.

A photograph of a scan on a screen, a screenshot, or an exported JPEG will not work, and the limitation is not cosmetic. Those formats carry brightness, not calibrated tissue density; they carry no slice position, no orientation, and no physical spacing. Reconstruction needs all of it. The validator refuses such input rather than producing something that looks plausible.

Does it tell me whether something is wrong with the lungs?

No. There is no disease classification, no abnormality detection, and no diagnostic model anywhere in the system, by design. It reconstructs and displays what is already in the scan.

The architecture leaves room for overlay layers to be added later, but nothing of that kind is implemented, and no placeholder is presented as though it works.

Is the 3D model actually built from my scan?

Yes. The surface is generated from that study's own voxels by marching cubes at its own physical spacing. No generic or prebuilt anatomical model is ever substituted.

If segmentation cannot confidently isolate the lungs, you get a stated failure with the reason — not a stand-in model.

How accurate is the reconstruction?

Geometry, spacing, and orientation are carried through from the source study rather than approximated, and the high-fidelity mesh is built at native resolution with no downsampling.

That said, segmentation is threshold-based. It will include air-filled structures that fall inside the same density range and can be affected by pathology that changes lung density. It has not been validated against expert manual segmentation, and no accuracy figure is claimed. Inspect the original slices alongside the model.

Why are there two quality modes?

Interactive downsamples the mask to at most 160 voxels per axis so rotation stays responsive on a large study. High Fidelity uses the full native resolution.

They are kept as separate representations. The simplified mesh is labelled wherever it appears and is never used for measurement or presented as the reference.

Does my imaging data leave the application?

No. Files are parsed in memory, and only six non-identifying technical fields are ever read from a dataset. No pixel data or metadata is transmitted to any external service, including AI and media-generation services.

Patient-identifying fields are never accessed at all — not read, not displayed, not stored.

Can I use this clinically?

No. This is a visualization and research tool. It is not a certified medical device, it has no regulatory clearance, and it makes no claim of clinical validation. Nothing it displays is a diagnosis.
